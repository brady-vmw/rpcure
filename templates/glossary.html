Glossary

Plain-English meanings for words you will see in trial records and research news.

{% set terms = [ ("Antisense oligonucleotide (AON)", "A short strand of genetic material injected into the eye that changes how a faulty gene's message is read. Sepofarsen and ultevursen are examples. It usually has to be repeated every few months."), ("Autosomal dominant", "An inheritance pattern where one changed copy of a gene is enough to cause disease. Parents are usually unaffected carriers."), ("Biallelic", "Changes in both copies of a gene. Luxturna, for example, requires biallelic RPE65 mutations."), ("BCVA (best-corrected visual acuity)", "How well you read an eye chart with your best glasses or contacts. Trials often report it in letters: 5 letters is about one line on the chart."), ("BLA (Biologics License Application)", "The application a company submits to the FDA to approve a gene or cell therapy. Acceptance means the FDA will review it, not that it is approved."), ("Cell therapy", "Treatment that injects living cells. In RP trials, cells are usually meant to protect remaining photoreceptors or, in early research, replace lost ones."), ("Charles Bonnet syndrome", "Seeing vivid images, patterns or people that are not there, caused by vision loss rather than mental illness. It is common with advanced RP and worth telling your doctor about."), ("Expanded access", "A way for some patients to get an unapproved treatment outside a trial, usually when no trial fits and the disease is serious."), ("Gene-agnostic (mutation-agnostic)", "Designed to work regardless of which gene causes your RP. Optogenetics, photoswitches, some cell therapies and antioxidant drugs are examples."), ("Gene therapy", "Delivering genetic material into retinal cells, usually with a harmless virus (AAV). Most replace a faulty gene; some add a helper gene instead."), ("Intravitreal injection", "An injection into the gel in the middle of the eye, done in a clinic. It often shows changes earlier than normal-light testing in RP."), ("Natural history study", "A study that follows people over time without treatment, to learn how the disease progresses. Its effect wears off, so it is repeated."), ("Pivotal trial", "The trial a company expects to use as its main evidence for approval, usually Phase 3 or a combined Phase 2/3."), ("Placebo and sham", "A placebo is an inactive look-alike treatment. Both let researchers compare treated and untreated groups fairly."), ("Primary endpoint", "The main measure a trial is judged on.
